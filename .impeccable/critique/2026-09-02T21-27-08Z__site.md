## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Viascope mostly passes the AI-slop test. The restrained explorer, mapped palette, crisp borders, and possibility-map metaphor create a credible product identity. The landing hero still carries template-like tells: an oversized grotesque headline, content-free “New” badge, tilted outlined demo panel, offset shadow, and repeated numbered pastel rows. The larger risk is product strangeness rather than decoration: unexplained precision, duplicate pathways, and finished-looking decision controls whose logic remains hidden.

**Deterministic scan:** The detector reported 132 findings: 113 advisories and 19 warnings. Of these, 115 are in `site/app/globals.css`, principally 51 literal colors, 45 font sizes, and 17 radii outside the documented token vocabulary. Seventeen font findings come from generated `.vinext` cache files and are noise. The side-tab finding is the possibility-map connector rather than a card stripe, and the rounded-border accent is the standard active-tab indicator; both are false positives. The remaining advisories indicate real design-system drift, but minified one-line CSS heavily inflates the apparent count.

**Visual overlays:** No reliable user-visible overlay was produced. Both routes rendered, but the available browser surface rejected mutable script injection, so the live detector server and overlay injection were correctly skipped. CLI results and rendered-route verification served as fallback evidence.

## Overall Impression

The landing page creates optimism and lowers commitment anxiety, while the explorer proves that Viascope can become a serious decision tool. The biggest opportunity is to make every recommendation explainable and progressively revealed. Today the interface moves from an inviting question to an authoritative ledger too quickly.

## What's Working

1. The landing page has one clear, credible starting action and sets useful boundaries: no account, public evidence, and no promise to decide for the learner.
2. Signal Violet, Action Lime, and restrained product surfaces create strong orientation without turning the app into a decorative dashboard.
3. The three-option shortlist supports judgment and tradeoffs rather than forcing a universal “best” answer.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Evidence looks precise but is not interpretable

**Why it matters:** Values such as 79/100, +7.7%, 561.7, and “student momentum” imply authority without defining calculation, unit, period, geography, uncertainty, or why they matter.

**Fix:** Replace “Signal” with a plain-language confidence label, format annual openings as people/jobs, add “Why this matched” disclosure per field, and expose source, period, geography, and score factors inline. Explain missing data rather than displaying only an em dash.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify site/app/explore`

### [P1] Failure and empty states are conflated with loading

**Why it matters:** A failed fetch sets the same empty array used by loading, leaving a learner waiting forever with no diagnosis or recovery.

**Fix:** Model loading, success, partial data, error, and no matches separately. Add a plain-language error, Retry control, source status, and live-region announcements.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden site/app/explore`

### [P1] The explorer abandons progressive disclosure

**Why it matters:** Learners move from one inviting question to four work choices, four priorities, nine credentials, 40+ states, four metrics, three tabs, and 36 pathways before seeing how the product helps.

**Fix:** Lead with 5–8 diverse, explained fields; group credential routes inside each field; defer location and advanced filters; reveal more results intentionally; make the three-question progress indicator reflect real completion.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill site/app/explore`

### [P2] Duplicate pathways undermine ranking quality

**Why it matters:** Repeated field and credential combinations make the output feel mechanically generated and force users to deduplicate options manually.

**Fix:** Make the study field the primary object, nest credential routes below it, deduplicate exact pairs, and support clusters or sorting such as strongest match, fastest growth, and shortest credential.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout site/app/explore`

### [P2] Accessibility state communication is incomplete

**Why it matters:** Tabs lack associated panels and arrow-key behavior, dynamic counts are not announced, tiny labels increase low-vision risk, and thin bars still carry important magnitude visually.

**Fix:** Implement the full tabs pattern, live regions, explicit score language, consistent focus states, mobile value labels, 200% zoom support, and color-blind-safe status communication.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit site`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan — uncertain first-time learner:** The landing action is clear, but the explorer immediately introduces “Signal,” “student momentum,” IPEDS, BLS, /100 scores, and percentages without context. The static “3 quick questions” strip promises guidance it does not provide, and the first 36 results offer no explanation of why one outranks another.

**Sam — accessibility-dependent learner:** Tabs lack complete keyboard semantics; loading and dynamic result changes are silent; several labels are only 0.62–0.72rem; mobile tables remove headers without replacing them with per-value labels; colored bars and green/red treatments remain too prominent as status carriers.

**Alex — experienced, time-constrained learner:** There is no sorting, field grouping, persistent/shareable scope, or efficient comparison. Reset does not fully reset, repeated routes slow scanning, and the arbitrary 36-result limit has no pagination or Show more action.

## Minor Observations

- “A student decision workspace” is narrower than the lifelong-learning audience.
- The “New” badge carries no product information.
- “Federal data loaded” needs freshness and a direct evidence link.
- College completion activity needs clearer separation from college quality or fit.
- The landing and explorer repeat numbered list rhythms.
- The CSS contains many literal values outside DESIGN.md, weakening future consistency.

## Questions to Consider

- If the /100 score disappeared, which plain-language evidence would still support a confident shortlist?
- Should the primary object be a field, a credential route, or a field–credential pair?
- Should the exploration endpoint produce advisor questions, a shareable comparison, institution outreach, or a saved research brief?
- Should the first impression explicitly name returning learners and career changers rather than “students”?
