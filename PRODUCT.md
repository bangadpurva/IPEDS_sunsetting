# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Viascope serves anyone considering school, training, or structured learning at any stage of life. Users may be choosing a first degree, changing careers, returning to education, adding a credential, or helping someone else evaluate options. They arrive with different levels of confidence, digital fluency, time, and familiarity with U.S. education and labor-market terminology.

Their core job is to understand what is happening around them—across programs, careers, institutions, costs, and labor demand—so they can narrow abundant options into a decision they can explain and act on.

## Product Purpose

Viascope turns public education and workforce evidence into an understandable decision workspace. It connects a person's interests and priorities to learning pathways, related occupations, and institutions while making the source, freshness, uncertainty, and limitations of important numbers visible.

Success has two connected stages: the user first develops a credible shortlist and better questions for advisors, families, or institutions; then they can progress toward selecting and applying to an appropriate program or learning opportunity. Viascope supports judgment rather than replacing it.

## Brand Personality

Optimistic, trustworthy, and empowering. The experience should feel current and culturally relevant without chasing novelty. Its language is direct, encouraging, inclusive, and free of institutional jargon. Airbnb is the primary product reference: approachable entry points, progressive disclosure, confident information hierarchy, strong comparison support, and an experience useful enough to share with others.

## Anti-references

Viascope must not resemble a legacy university portal, a dense government-data dashboard, a high-pressure admissions funnel, or a personality quiz that presents an opaque answer as destiny. It must not infantilize adult learners, bury uncertainty, imply guaranteed outcomes, or require users to understand research terminology before receiving value. Avoid generic AI-product aesthetics, decorative dashboards, information overload, and inaccessible color-only meaning.

## Design Principles

1. **Begin with the person's question.** Let users start in their own words, then progressively introduce structure and terminology.
2. **Make evidence understandable.** Pair every important signal with plain-language meaning, provenance, freshness, and visible uncertainty.
3. **Support decisions, not rankings.** Help users compare tradeoffs and build a defensible shortlist instead of declaring one universal best path.
4. **Welcome every learning stage.** Use inclusive language and workflows that work for first-time, returning, career-changing, and continuing learners.
5. **Earn sharing through usefulness.** Produce clear comparisons and next steps that users naturally want to discuss with other people.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across the responsive web experience. All essential workflows must support keyboard navigation, visible focus, screen readers, zoom, reduced motion, and color-blind-safe signals. Text and interactive controls require compliant contrast; color never carries meaning alone. Use plain language, explain unfamiliar education and labor terms in context, accommodate low-confidence users, and avoid assumptions about age, income, family support, prior education, or ability.
